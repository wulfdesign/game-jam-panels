🎙️ Panel Question 2 • The Crucible • Failing Forward

Other Jams & The 9th Wall: The "Glorious Failure"

The greatest lessons in game development come from the catastrophic 2:00 AM disasters.

💬 Question 2 • The Crucible "We've all had jams where the scope crept, the engine crashed, or the game just wasn't fun. Tell us about your hardest game jam. Was it a glorious failure, and what was the most valuable thing you learned from a jam that didn't go as planned?"
Intake Submissions & Lore

"What is the jam where everything went completely sideways?"

• Dr. Evie Powell (UNCC GGJ): "Did you murder your wife" — a game where you had to stash a body in a variety of places so the police wouldn't suspect you while you got to a boat (you didn't murder your wife btw). It became a reddit urban legend in their local subreddit when someone saw the postmortem on the projector outside of the venue!

• Larry James (GGJ 2015): Tale Spinner — A network multiplayer card game that ran out of runway and turned into a frantic scramble having it ready 10 minutes before judging with a print-and-play paper card as backup!

The Takeaway

Why Game Jams Make Failure Safe

In commercial development, failure costs budgets, contracts, and years of stress. In a game jam, failure costs only 48 hours—and leaves you with an unforgettable war story and an invaluable lesson you will carry for the rest of your career.

Learn Rather Than Win
